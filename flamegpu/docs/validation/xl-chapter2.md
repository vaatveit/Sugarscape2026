# xl Chapter II: the GPU against Python

Generated by `python -m bridge.xl_validate` (xl design, section 5). Each metric is compared at 10%, 25%, 50% and 100% of the run's periods, as the GPU's seed mean against Python's.

- **Gap:** |GPU − Python| as a percentage of the metric's scale, the largest |Python mean| over the run.
- **Spread:** Welch's t over seeds.
- A comparison **disagrees** when its gap is over 1% *and* |t| > 3 (or there is no spread on both sides, so no t).
- A metric undefined (nan) in every seed on one side only is **undefined on one side**, listed with the disagreements. One undefined on both sides, or whose Python scale is 0, is not judged. Seeds where a metric is undefined are left out of its means; the n columns count the seeds that remain.

**Counts:** 14 disagree, 0 undefined on one side, 190 agree, 36 not judged.

**Smoke test** (II-5/m3/v10.5 at xl, visions 10–200, GPU only): finished.

## Reading these results

*Written by hand on 2026-10-25, after replacement moved to the device and metrics began to be gathered in bulk
(spec 2026-10-25-gpu-replacement-metrics). Regenerating the report drops this section.* The runs use the look stride
(main 0b6b937). Only II-4-gini's GPU runs changed since the previous report. Every comparison row of the other
experiments is byte-identical to that report's, because their runs are.

- **II-4, II-4-gini and II-7 agree on every judged comparison** at all 4 checkpoints.
  - II-4's mean neighbours is within 0.6% at those checkpoints, against the GPU's +7% at 50×50 (Phase 1, which named
    it a measurement difference). Between checkpoints the difference still shows where many agents move at once:
    II-4's neighbours are +30% at t = 1 (series below).
  - II-4-gini's mean neighbours runs 0.2–0.9% above Python's at the checkpoints. That is the same direction as II-4's
    at 50×50. Its t reaches 3.38 at t = 500, but the gap stays under the 1% threshold.
  - **Replacement on the device against replacement on the host**, the GPU with each, 30 xl seeds of II-4-gini:
    - the 7 metrics compared (mean neighbours, Gini, mean sugar, vision and metabolism, on-peak fraction, moved) are
      within 0.34% at t = 50, 40–60, 100–200, 250 and 400–500;
    - the largest |t| is 2.97 (mean metabolism +0.23%), at the single period t = 250, where the 100–200 and
      400–500 windows show no shift;
    - this is consistent with chance, with no shift above about 0.3%. The rule's distribution is unchanged.
- **II-6 holds 13 of the 14 disagreements, most of them consistent with fewer agents leaving the packed starting
  block on the GPU.**
  - North-east fraction −10 to −15%, north fraction −3 to −4%, on-peak fraction +1.5%, Gini −1.9% at t = 75, and
    mean sugar −2.7% at t = 300. (`north_frac_high_vision` counts agents with vision ≥ 5 in regular units, which at
    xl is every agent, so here it is the north fraction of all agents.) Nothing here derives the signs of the Gini
    and mean-sugar gaps from the spread.
  - It is a lasting difference, not a delay. The GPU's north-east fraction is ahead at first (+6 to +10% at t =
    4–7), then −10% by t = 10, and stays near −15% from t ≈ 12 to t = 300.
  - Attributed, not proven, to a documented design difference. II-6 starts with one agent on every site of the
    block. In Python, an agent late in the period's order sees the sites that earlier agents left. On the GPU,
    every agent scans the start-of-period positions, so an agent in the block's interior sees only occupied sites,
    stays put, and doesn't scan again that period.
  - Phase 1 saw the same direction at 50×50: the NE-peak proxy was 0.046 on the GPU against 0.049 in Python, and
    0.010 against 0.013 for II-6-measured. Status: attributed to a known difference; its size is open.
- **II-8: one disagreement, mean neighbours at t = 200, −5.4%.** II-8 is the only experiment with pollution
  (from t = 50) and diffusion (from t = 100). The per-period seed means of mean neighbours show two effects:
  - **Positive spikes wherever many agents move at once:** +30% at t = 1; +3% at t = 3 and t = 5; +3 to +8% over
    t = 55–65, just after pollution starts, peaking at +8.0% at t = 55; about +2% at t = 70; and +45% at t = 102,
    where the GPU's count jumps from 0.43 to about 1.2 one period before Python's does. These fit the difference
    Phase 1 named: the GPU counts neighbours after everyone has moved, while Python counts each agent's right after
    its own move. II-4 shows the same at t = 1.
  - **A steady shortfall after diffusion starts:** −1.5% at t = 103, −4.5% by t = 110, then between −4.5% and −8.0%
    (the lowest at t = 136) to the end of the run. Its sign is the opposite of the counting difference, so it is a
    separate effect. Status: **open.** Phase 1's open II-8 pollution shift (−3%) may be related.
  - Diffusion itself agrees with Python within 1e-4: the parity case `diffusion_passes_xl` runs 100 passes on a
    20×20 map. Neither side records pollution as a metric, so at xl the 100-pass diffusion is checked only through
    how agents move.
  - Every other II-8 metric agrees at every checkpoint.
- **Not judged (36)** are metrics undefined on both sides. `summer_north` is undefined without seasons.
  `north_frac_low_vision` defines low vision in regular units, so no xl agent, whose vision is at least 10, has it.
- **Timings** (the fast build, one seed at a time, from `timing.csv`).
  - **II-4-gini on the GPU** now steps in 0.83 ms per period against II-4's 0.71 ms; the previous report had 9.1 ms.
    A profile of the old code (200 periods, 6.3 ms per period) put 4.7 ms of each period in the host-side
    `replace_dead`. Newcomers are now born and placed on the GPU.
  - **Runs without replacement step faster than in the previous report** (II-4 1.21 → 0.71 ms, II-6 1.23 → 0.82 ms,
    II-7 1.04 → 0.70 ms, II-8 4.30 → 3.01 ms), and GPU setup fell by a similar share (0.28 → 0.20 s). Their outputs
    are unchanged. Timing the old and new binaries now, alternately, 200 xl periods each:
    - without `--out` they step at the same speed (II-4 0.68–0.70 against 0.63–0.68 ms, II-6 0.83–0.84 against
      0.82–0.84 ms);
    - with `--out`, the old binary is 0.10–0.13 ms per period slower (II-4 0.84 against 0.73 ms, II-6 0.94–0.97
      against 0.84–0.86 ms).

    So the old per-agent metrics read, in the end-of-period step function, did leak into the timed periods. Why
    wasn't profiled. The rest of the change since the previous report, setup included, comes from different run
    conditions.
  - **Metrics** (`metrics_s` per row) fell from 9.6–13.5 ms to 0.86–1.09 ms. Rows are now gathered between periods
    as whole columns. About 0.8 ms of each row is computing it (mostly the Gini sort), which stays on the host so the
    rows are byte-identical.
  - **II-8 on the GPU** takes 3.0 ms per period, likely the 100 diffusion passes it runs every period from t = 100.
  - **GPU setup** is about 0.20 s per seed. Every seed is a fresh process that starts CUDA.
  - **Python's** timings come from `results-xl/`, taken with `--jobs 10` sharing the processor, so read them as upper
    bounds. Its II-4-gini figure, 22.6 s per period, predates main's commit 77ffcb6, which made Python's replacement
    fast. `docs/xl.md` now gives 1.13 s per period, against II-4's 1.17 s, timed together over t = 3–7. That commit
    left Python's seed CSVs byte-identical, so the comparisons above stand.
